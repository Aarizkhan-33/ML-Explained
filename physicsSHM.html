The Shadow Analogy: Circle Meets Oscillation

A particle moving in a circle casts a shadow that vibrates back and forth in Simple Harmonic Motion.

Definition — Circular Motion & S.H.M.

When a particle of mass m whirls in a horizontal circle of radius r with uniform angular velocity ω, a distant parallel light source casts a shadow of the particle onto a flat screen or wall.

While the real particle travels smoothly in a 2D circle at constant speed, its 1D shadow moves back and forth along a straight line—slowing down at the edges, speeding up through the center, and executing Simple Harmonic Motion (S.H.M.).

Why Study S.H.M. Using a Circle?

  • Circular motion is easy to track using a single steady angle θ = ωt.
  • Projecting that circular motion onto a diameter gives us exact formulas for displacement (x), velocity (vx), acceleration (ax), time period (T), and phase (φ) without needing calculus!

Turntable & Overhead Lamps Visual

Overhead lamps project circular motion of mass m onto a screen. The shadow oscillates between -r and +r about mean position O.

The Reference Circle & Standard Notation

Replacing the physical lamps with clean geometry: projecting point P onto diameter AB.

Reference Circle Diagram

Particle P moves counterclockwise on a circle of radius r. Foot of perpendicular Q moves back and forth along diameter AB.

Core Symbols (Term-by-Term)

  • P (Reference Particle): Mass m rotating counterclockwise on the circle at uniform angular velocity ω.
  • Q (Projection / Shadow): The foot of the perpendicular dropped from P onto diameter AB. Q executes S.H.M.
  • O (Mean Position): Center of the circle where displacement x = 0.
  • r (Radius = Amplitude x0): The maximum distance Q can reach from O (at points A and B).

Connecting Angle to Displacement

In right-angled triangle ΔOQP, the horizontal distance of projection Q from the center O is the adjacent side:

cos θ = xr   &implies;   x = r cos θ = r cos(ωt)

Because θ increases steadily as θ = ωt, the projection x oscillates smoothly between +r and -r.

What is Amplitude (x0)?

Amplitude is the maximum displacement of the oscillating projection Q from the mean position O on either side. Since P never moves farther horizontally than the circle's radius r, the radius of the reference circle IS the amplitude of S.H.M. (r = x0).

Why Constant Speed ≠ Constant Projection Speed

Crucial Distinction

Particle P moves along the circular rim at a constant tangential speed v = rω. However, its shadow Q does NOT move at constant speed!

  • Near the edges (A or B), P moves almost vertically (perpendicular to the screen), so its horizontal shadow Q momentarily stops (vx = 0).
  • Near the center (O), P moves horizontally (parallel to the screen), so Q zips past at full speed (vx = rω).

Visualizing Projection Q at Three Key Angles

Watch how displacement x, velocity vx, and acceleration ax trade off as P rotates.

Case 1: Extreme Position (θ = 0°)

x = +r,  vx = 0,  ax = -ω²r (Max)

Q is at the right extreme A. Motion reverses here, so velocity is zero while restoring acceleration pulling back toward O is maximum.

Case 2: Intermediate (θ = 60°)

x = +0.5r,  vx = -0.866 rω,  ax = -0.5ω²r

As P climbs from A toward top, Q moves left toward O. Both velocity and restoring acceleration point left (negative x).

Case 3: Mean Position (θ = 90°)

x = 0,  vx = -rω (Max),  ax = 0

P is directly above O. Shadow Q passes through equilibrium at maximum speed, and net horizontal acceleration is momentarily zero.

Proof That Projection Q Executes S.H.M.

Resolving the centripetal acceleration of P along the diameter proves ax ∝ -x.

ac = v²r = rω²   (directed toward O)
ax = -ac cos θ = -rω² cos θ
Since cos θ = x / r:
ax = -rω² (xr) = -ω² x
Why the Minus Sign?

Displacement x is measured outward from the mean position O, while centripetal acceleration ac always points inward toward O. Therefore, the horizontal component ax always points opposite to x (restoring Q back toward equilibrium).

Because ω is constant, ω² is constant, giving ax = -(constant) x, or ax ∝ -x — the exact defining condition of Simple Harmonic Motion!

Resolving Centripetal Acceleration

In ΔOQP, cos θ = x / r. Vector ac points inward from P to O, so its horizontal projection ax = ac cos θ always points toward O.

Time Period (T) and Frequency (f) from Circular Motion

One complete circular revolution of P corresponds to exactly one complete vibration of Q.

1. Time Period (T)

Definition: The time required for projection Q to complete one full back-and-forth vibration (e.g., A → O → B → O → A).

In that same time, particle P sweeps through one full circle of angle 2π radians (arc length s = 2πr) at angular velocity ω:

T = 2πω

2. Frequency (f) & Angular Velocity (ω)

Definition: The number of complete vibrations executed per second (unit: Hertz, Hz). It is the reciprocal of Time Period:

f = 1T = ω2π   &implies;   ω = 2πf

One Full Vibration = One Revolution

Total circular path s = 2πr swept in time t = T corresponds to one complete oscillation of Q from +r to -r and back.

Velocity of Projection in S.H.M.

Resolving tangential circular velocity v = rω along the diameter AB.

Step-by-Step Derivation

Particle P moves tangent to the circle with constant speed v = rω. Resolving vector v into rectangular components:

  • vx = v sin θ (horizontal component along diameter AB — moves projection Q)
  • vy = v cos θ (vertical component — perpendicular to AB, ignored by Q)
From sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and cos θ = x / r:
sin θ = √(1 - x²/r²) = √(r² - x²)r
Substitute sin θ and v = rω into vx = v sin θ:
vx = (rω) · √(r² - x²)r = ω √(r² - x²)
In amplitude notation (r = x0):  v = ω √(x0² - x²)

Velocity Resolution & Extremes Check

Tangential velocity v is perpendicular to radius OP, making angle (90° - θ) with x-axis, so its horizontal component is v sin θ.

Checking the Two Extremes

• At Mean Position (x = 0): vmax = ω√(r² - 0) = rω

• At Extreme Positions (x = ±r): vmin = ω√(r² - r²) = 0

Waveform of S.H.M. & Phase (θ = ωt)

Unrolling circular motion against time produces the classic sine/cosine wave.

Left: Particle P rotating on reference circle of radius r = x0. Right: Projection x plotted against θ = ωt traces a smooth sinusoidal curve over period T (2π radians).
What is Phase (θ = ωt + φ)?

Phase is the angle θ = ωt (or more generally ωt + φ) that specifies BOTH the instantaneous displacement x AND the direction of motion of the oscillating particle at any instant.

Reading the Waveform

  • Starting at mean position (y = r sin ωt) vs starting at extreme position (x = r cos ωt) simply shifts the wave by a phase constant φ = 90° (π/2 rad).
  • Every full cycle of 2π radians spans exactly one Time Period T.

Exam Recap: Circular Motion & S.H.M. Cheat-Sheet

All core equations, extremes, and common traps in one place.

1. Core Equations

  • Displacement: x = r cos(ωt)
  • Acceleration: ax = -ω²x
  • Velocity: vx = ω √(r² - x²)
  • Period & Freq: T = 2π/ω,  ω = 2πf

2. Mean vs. Extreme Table

  • At Mean (x = 0): vx is MAX (rω), ax = 0
  • At Extreme (x = ±r): vx = 0, |ax| is MAX (ω²r)
  • Direction: ax ALWAYS points toward O (opposite to x).

3. Three Common Mistakes

  • Dropping the minus sign: Writing ax = ω²x misses why the motion is restoring S.H.M.
  • Mixing sin & cos: x and ax use cos θ, while vx uses sin θ (velocity is 90° ahead of displacement).
  • Degrees vs. Radians: Always convert rpm or degrees into rad/s (ω = 2πf) before computing formulas.
